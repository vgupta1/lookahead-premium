\section{Constructing the Frame}
\label{sec:ECSearch}

Any claim about what a literature does requires a stated denominator, and this appendix is ours. We
do not attempt a systematic review. We build a frame that is \emph{purposive but reproducible}: a
search that is stated precisely enough to be replayed, from two sources chosen because they fail in
different directions.

The first source is the reference list of each of two recent surveys of the area -- \citet{mandi2024decision}
and \citet{sadana2025survey} -- taking every work they cite as a candidate. Reference lists reach
back in time and across subfields, and they are curated by authors other than us, which is their
value here. Their weakness is that they stop at their own publication dates and inherit whatever
their authors chose to read. The second source is a keyword search of eleven venues over
2023--September 2026. A database query reaches what the surveys missed or postdate, and it applies a
stated rule rather than a judgement; its weakness is that it finds only what shares vocabulary with
the query. Neither source alone would be adequate, and we quantify the second's incompleteness
below.

\subsection{Sources, Venues and Window}

The eleven venues are NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, IJCAI and AISTATS on the machine-learning side;
\emph{Operations Research}, \emph{Management Science}, \emph{Manufacturing \& Service Operations
Management} and the \emph{INFORMS Journal on Computing} on the operations side; and CPAIOR, which
carries much of the constraint-programming work in this area. Notable exclusions are deliberate:
\emph{EJOR}, which is high-volume and already reached through \citet{sadana2025survey}, together
with \emph{JMLR}, \emph{TMLR}, \emph{Mathematics of Operations Research}, \emph{Production and
Operations Management} and \emph{Naval Research Logistics}. The frame is bounded by design, and we
disclose the boundary rather than implying coverage we do not have.

Preprints enter only by being cited in a survey. We ran no arXiv or OpenReview sweep, because such a
search is not a stable frame -- the result depends on the day it is run -- which is the exact
objection this appendix exists to answer.

The window overlaps the surveys' own coverage by roughly a year, and that overlap is not waste. It
supplies a known answer against which the search can be scored, and we report that score below.

\subsection{The Reference Lists}

Both bibliographies were parsed from the published PDFs rather than taken from a bibliographic
graph, and the difference matters. For the same two articles, OpenAlex returned \(129\) referenced
works against the \(153\) that \citet{mandi2024decision} prints -- \(16\%\) short, with eighteen dead
identifiers and at least three matcher false positives -- and \(209\) against the \(191\) printed by
\citet{sadana2025survey}. Crossref holds no references at all for the first. Where an independent
check was available it agreed with the parse: the publisher's Crossref deposit for the second lists
\(191\), exactly what the parse returned. We also checked each parsed list for alphabetical
monotonicity, which is what catches the merged and split entries that hanging-indent parsing
produces, and found no violations in either.

Merging the two lists is where the care is required. \(31\) works match on normalized title. A
further seven are the same work printed differently in the two bibliographies -- hyphenation lost in
extraction (\texttt{decisionmaking}), an arXiv title against the proceedings title of the same
paper, a preprint year against a published one -- and one work appears twice within a single
bibliography, as a conference paper and as the journal article it became. Two entries were also
restored after a parse collision: both print a volume number before the title, both reduced to the
title \texttt{Vol}, and one was silently absorbed into the other.

We record the lesson because it generalizes. Title matching failed here in both directions at once,
producing one false merge and missing seven true pairs, roughly a fifth of the overlap. A match
between two reference lists needs a review of near misses and of short or degenerate titles, not
merely a pass over exact hits. After these corrections -- each recorded in the frame file itself,
with its reason -- the reference lists contribute \(313\) printed entries, \(305\) distinct works,
of which \(38\) are cited by both surveys.

That last number is worth a sentence of its own. Two surveys of the same subject, one written from
the machine-learning side and one from the operations side, share about one work in eight.

\subsection{The Query}

The search terms are recall-oriented, with precision left to the screening stages
(\cref{sec:ECVenueScreen}): the vocabulary of decision-focused learning and predict-then-optimize,
of contextual and prescriptive optimization, of end-to-end and task-based learning, and of the
differentiable-optimization machinery this literature builds on. Three families of term were
considered and rejected. \emph{Learning to optimize} denotes a different field and is very noisy;
bare \emph{regret} paired with predict or optimize returns most of the bandit literature; and the
oracle-naming terms -- \emph{full-information optimum}, \emph{perfect foresight}, \emph{wait-and-see}
-- target our object directly but pull in large volumes of reinforcement-learning work. The last of
these is worth revisiting as a validation probe rather than as a search term.

The query of record was run once, in Scopus Advanced Search, on 2026-09-16:

\begin{verbatim}
TITLE-ABS-KEY (
    "decision-focused learning" OR "decision focused learning"
 OR ( predict* W/3 optimi* )
 OR "predict-then-optimize" OR "predict then optimize"
 OR "predict and optimize" OR "predict+optimize"
 OR "prediction and optimization"
 OR "smart predict"
 OR "contextual optimization" OR "contextual stochastic optimization"
 OR "integrated learning and optimization" OR "learning-integrated optimization"
 OR "decision-aware learning"
 OR "task-based learning" OR "task loss"
 OR "decision loss" OR "decision losses"
 OR "differentiable optimization" OR "prescriptive analytics"
 OR "predictive to prescriptive"
 OR "feature-based newsvendor" OR "data-driven newsvendor"
 OR ( "end-to-end learning" AND ( optimization OR decision ) )
 OR "optimization layer" OR "optimization layers"
 OR "unrolled solver*" OR "folded optimization" OR "differentiable combinatorial"
 OR ( differentiable W/2 ( solver* OR algorithm* OR "shortest path*" ) )
 OR ( "implicit differentiation"
      AND ( stochastic OR uncertain* OR predict* OR contextual ) )
 OR ( "data-driven optimization"
      AND ( context* OR covariate OR "side information" OR predict* ) )
 OR ( ( "side information" OR "covariate information" )
      AND ( optimi* OR decision OR prescriptive ) )
)
AND PUBYEAR > 2022
AND (
    SRCTITLE ( "Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems" )
 OR SRCTITLE ( "Proceedings of Machine Learning Research" )
 OR SRCTITLE ( "*AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence*" )
 OR SRCTITLE ( "IJCAI International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence" )
 OR SRCTITLE ( "*Learning Representations*" )
 OR SRCTITLE ( "Lecture Notes in Computer Science*" )
 OR SRCTITLE ( "Operations Research" )
 OR SRCTITLE ( "Management Science" )
 OR SRCTITLE ( "Manufacturing and Service Operations Management" )
 OR SRCTITLE ( "INFORMS Journal on Computing" )
)
\end{verbatim}

It returns \(829\) records, of which \(379\) are at the eleven venues in the window. Venue membership
is decided afterwards, in versioned code, rather than by tightening the query: the source-title
clauses above are deliberately loose, because several venues cannot be identified from the source
title at all, and a decision made in a search box is neither inspectable nor reproducible.

Four indexing behaviours cost us records before they were found, and we report them because they are
invisible from a result set and will recur for anyone running this search. AAAI 2023 sits under a
source title carrying an ordinal inside the string, so an exact-phrase clause returns \emph{zero}
AAAI 2023 papers -- a convincing-looking coverage gap; the wildcard above recovers eighteen. ICML
and AISTATS share the single source title \emph{Proceedings of Machine Learning Research} with UAI,
CoRL, COLT and L4DC, and are separable only by conference name; CPAIOR is inside Springer's LNCS
series, shared with dozens of unrelated conferences, and is likewise identifiable only by conference
name. Scopus silently matches nothing for a quoted phrase containing \texttt{+}, which is how a
known in-scope paper was lost for two revisions. And \texttt{TITLE-ABS-KEY} searches keyword fields:
\(167\) of the \(379\) records carry no matching phrase in their title or abstract and matched on
author or index keywords, so a local regular expression over titles and abstracts does \emph{not}
reproduce this record set.

The query reached its current form through five revisions, each scored against the known answer
described next, recovering \(4\), \(9\), \(13\), \(14\) and \(14\) of the \(19\) scoring papers in
turn. One revision is instructive: replacing three literal phrases with a proximity operator that
appeared to subsume them lost a record, because the two forms behave differently against keyword
fields. Redundant disjuncts are free. Add terms; do not remove them.

\subsection{Search Recall}

Because the window overlaps the surveys' coverage, the frame contains a set of papers the search
should have found: the \(19\) works cited by a survey that were published at one of the eleven
venues in 2023. The search returns \(14\) of them, a recall of \(74\%\).

We report this rather than smoothing it over, with two qualifications. The scoring set has \(19\)
members, so the interval around \(74\%\) is wide. And a miss here is a failure of the search, not a
gap in the corpus: every one of the five is in the frame through a survey. What the misses share is
instructive -- a pricing paper in \emph{MSOM}, a learned-surrogate paper for combinatorial problems,
two perturbation-based gradient-estimation papers and a satisfiability-layer paper -- namely almost
no vocabulary in common with the terms this literature uses about itself. Keyword search will not
reach \(100\%\) recall on this literature, and that is the argument for building the frame from two
sources with different failure modes rather than for tuning the query further.

Of the \(379\) records, \(28\) are works a survey already cites and \(351\) are new to the frame.

\subsection{Replication}

Elsevier's terms restrict redistributing downloaded records, and the abstracts in them are publisher
copyright, so the raw export is not part of the replication package. What is committed is the query
above, the export date, the code that assigns venues and applies the window, and, for all \(379\)
records, the Scopus identifier, DOI, title, authors, venue and year. Anyone with Scopus access can
recover the exact record set from those identifiers, or re-run the query and compare. The reference
lists of the two surveys are committed in full, as are the screening labels of
\cref{sec:ECVenueScreen} and \cref{sec:ECSurveyScreen} and the grounds recorded for each.

\section{Screening the Venue Papers}
\label{sec:ECVenueScreen}

The venue sweep returns \(379\) records at the eleven venues. Most are not about contextual
optimization at all: the query is deliberately recall-oriented (\cref{sec:ECSearch}), and terms such
as \emph{task loss} and \emph{differentiable optimization} are common in unrelated machine-learning
work. Before obtaining full texts we therefore apply a single coarse filter, which we call
\emph{abstract-stage triage}.

We stress at the outset what this stage does \emph{not} do. It does not apply the inclusion rules of
\cref{sec:ECInclusion}, and it does not determine whether a paper studies a stochastic parameter. It
decides only which full texts are worth obtaining. Every substantive classification in this audit is
made from the full text, for reasons documented below.

\subsection{Why the Filter Is Not a Keyword Rule}

The natural implementation is a keyword or topic rule applied to the abstract, and we initially
adopted one: exclude a paper when its abstract indicates that no parameter is predicted, so that the
optimization instance is fully observed and the learning task is to solve it faster. Papers of this
kind -- amortized optimization, learned warm starts, neural combinatorial solvers -- are genuinely
out of scope, since with no unobserved \(Y\) there are no two oracles and no premium.

That rule fails systematically, and it fails on exactly the papers we can least afford to lose. We
tested it against twelve papers already known to be in scope, judging each from its abstract alone.
Four of the twelve would have been excluded: \citet{berthet2020learning},
\citet{poganvcic2020differentiation}, \citet{sahoo2023backpropagation} and
\citet{niepert2021implicit}. Each describes its
\emph{method} -- differentiating through a combinatorial solver -- and leaves the \emph{setting}, in
which a cost vector is predicted from context, to its experiments. Their abstracts contain no
language about prediction at all.

Abstracts at machine-learning venues foreground methodological contribution, so the papers most
likely to be misread are precisely those in the differentiable-optimization lineage that the search
was widened to capture. A referee has no way to
detect such an exclusion from the published record, since an excluded paper leaves no trace. We
therefore adopt a rule that never asks whether anything is predicted.

\subsection{The Triage Rule}

A record is retained when both of the following hold: an optimization or decision problem is the
object of the work, and a learned component appears in the same pipeline. A record is excluded when
there is no decision problem at all, when the only optimization is machinery for training or
compressing a model (hyperparameter search, architecture search, pruning, distillation), or when the
work is numerical simulation of a physical system. Records that cannot be judged are retained.

The asymmetry is deliberate. A record wrongly retained costs one full-text retrieval; a record
wrongly excluded is invisible for the remainder of the study.

\subsection{Implementation and Reproducibility}

The rule requires reading an abstract for what a paper is about, which no lexical pattern captures.
We implement it as a language-model classifier under a fixed prompt, and treat the prompt as part of
the method rather than as an implementation detail: it is versioned in the replication repository,
states the criterion as enumerated inclusions and exclusions rather than as an abstract principle,
and carries five worked examples with their reasoning, among them \citet{berthet2020learning} as a
retained record. Two instructions in the prompt exist solely to prevent the failure above, and each
records the evidence that motivated it.

The model identifier is pinned and decoding is greedy. For each record we store the assigned
bucket, a one-sentence rationale in the model's own words, a self-reported confidence, the model
identifier, a hash of the prompt, and the run date. The rationale is what makes the stage auditable:
a reader disagreeing with a particular exclusion can see the stated ground for it.

Because greedy decoding does not make a language model deterministic, every record is classified
three times and assigned its majority bucket, with the three individual buckets retained so that a
split is visible rather than silently resolved. Re-classifying one hundred records under the pinned
model returned the assigned bucket in all \(100\) cases, with the stated rationale identical in
\(76\). Note what this compares: a fresh single classification against the three-run majority of
record, which is the quantity that enters the labels we use. It concerns decoding variation alone,
and we are explicit below that it is not the quantity a reader should care most about.

We are careful not to overclaim here. Greedy decoding reduces run-to-run variation but does not
eliminate it, and a provider-side model revision can move a borderline record. This is why the stage
is validated on every execution against a labelled control set of papers known to be in scope,
four of which -- the four named above -- carry no prediction language in their abstracts and are the
binding test; the pipeline halts if any control record is excluded. We report the control-set result
alongside the triage counts.

\subsection{Execution and Outcome}

We executed the stage once on the \(379\) sweep records, under the pinned model identifier
\texttt{claude-opus-4-5-20251101}. The three runs agreed unanimously on \(373\) records. Three
records are proceedings front matter, removed by a prior rule rather than by the classifier. Three
records split, and were ruled by hand; in all three the hand ruling confirmed the majority bucket, so
they are recorded as adjudications rather than as corrections.

A fourth record was re-ruled. It had been examined during the design phase described below, under a
version of the prompt that was subsequently revised; the revision moved it from excluded to retained,
and we judged that a verdict rendered against a superseded specification could not be carried
forward unexamined. We re-read it under the current rule and excluded it. We record this because the
general point governs any audit of this kind: a human verdict is a verdict about a stated rule, and
it expires when the rule changes.

After adjudication the stage retains \(158\) records and excludes \(221\). Adjudications are stored
in a separate file and applied programmatically, so the classifier's output is never edited and every
human override is visible as a difference between two files. No record belonging to the survey reference list --
the references of the two surveys from which the sampling frame is built (\cref{sec:ECSearch}) -- was
excluded. This is a consistency check rather than a validation, since a survey's reference list is
not a gold standard for our inclusion rules, but a failure here would have been informative.

\subsection{Validating the Excluded Records}

Triage errors in the retaining direction correct themselves at full text. Errors in the excluding
direction do not, and they are therefore the quantity we measure. We measure them in two phases, and
we keep the phases separate.

\textbf{Design.} The first phase reports no statistic. We reviewed every excluded record whose
recorded confidence was below high, together with every excluded record at an operations-research
venue, where off-topic noise is rare: thirteen in all. Twelve confirmed the exclusion. The thirteenth
was a paper whose learned component is an amortized solver for optimal transport, excluded on the
stated ground that optimal transport there is a computational tool rather than a decision problem.
That is a failure to follow the rule rather than a defect in the rule, and it exposed an ambiguity in
how the rule was stated: the requirement of a learned component was being read as requiring a
predictor of problem \emph{data}, so a learned \emph{solver} did not register as satisfying it. We
revised the prompt to say that the learned component may be the solver itself, keeping both
requirements in force -- a faster classical algorithm with nothing learned remains excluded, as does
numerical simulation of a physical system with no decision problem.

That revision moved \(15\) of the \(379\) records, which deserves comment: a filter whose output
shifts under a small change to its instructions invites the suspicion that it is unstable. The prompt
\emph{is} the operational definition of the rule, so a revision that moved nothing would mean it had
not taken effect. The question is instead whether the records that moved are the ones the revision
was about. Comparing the runs before and after, all fifteen lie on the boundary the revision redrew:
six newly retained (amortized solvers for optimal transport and for mixed-integer programs, learned
molecular docking), six newly excluded (bilevel optimization used as training machinery,
treatment-effect estimation, learned navigation with no optimization problem formulated), and three
moving through the undecided bucket. None lie elsewhere. We read this as a targeted edit rather than
as drift.

\textbf{Measurement.} With the prompt frozen, we drew a fresh random sample of \(50\) of the \(221\)
excluded records under a recorded seed, and one author classified them by hand from title, venue,
year and abstract alone, with the model's bucket, rationale and confidence withheld so that the
reading was independent of them. The draw, the reading sheet and the recorded verdicts are in the
replication repository, so the sample is reproducible rather than merely described. All \(50\) were
confirmed excluded. Since the sample is a substantial fraction of a finite population, the
appropriate bound is hypergeometric rather than binomial: with \(95\%\) confidence, at most \(11\) of
the \(221\) excluded records are wrongly excluded. (The corresponding Clopper--Pearson bounds, which
discard the finite-population information, are \(5.8\%\) one-sided, or \(12.9\) records, and
\(7.1\%\) two-sided.)

The two phases cannot be pooled. Targeted sampling deliberately oversamples where errors are
expected, so its error rate does not estimate the rate in the excluded pile; and a sample that drove
a revision of the instrument is contaminated as a measurement of it.

\subsection{Scope of the Claim}

Triage is a retrieval decision and we claim no more for it. The bound above is a bound on
disagreement between the classifier and one reader applying the written rule. It is not evidence that
the rule is the right one, which is argued in \cref{sec:ECInclusion} rather than estimated, and it is
not a claim about language-model triage in general: it is a statement about this prompt, at the
recorded hash, under the recorded model. We have not measured sensitivity to paraphrases of the
prompt that preserve the rule. We did pilot the rule against a smaller model of the same family
before pinning the one above, and that pilot is worth reporting. Under the identical frozen prompt
the smaller model differed on \(23\) of the \(379\) records, \(17\) of them exclusions the pinned
model did not make, and its own stated grounds say what went wrong: in thirteen of the seventeen it
recorded that no optimization or decision problem was the object of the work, in papers where the
optimization plainly is the object -- Bayesian optimization, model predictive control, inverse
optimization, performative prediction, learned optimal transport. This is the failure the revision
described above was written against, reappearing under the revised wording that was meant to close
it: the requirement of a learned component was again being read as a requirement that problem
\emph{data} be predicted, so an optimization that is itself the object of the work registered as
machinery for something else. The smaller model passed the control set, \(12/12\), under the prompt
against which it was piloted, and we do not report that as evidence in its favour: a model can pass
a twelve-paper control set and still misread the rule at the boundary in the way just described.
The control set is a floor -- it catches a model that has stopped recognizing the papers this stage
exists to protect -- and not a test of whether a model applies the rule where the rule is hard. That
is why the identifier is pinned rather than merely validated, and why every count reported here is
tied to a fixed identifier and a fixed prompt hash. Perhaps the most honest statement is that
this stage is designed so that its failures are recoverable, not so that it is correct: every
subsequent stage of the audit works from full texts, and the screening log records a ground for
each exclusion so that a reader may disagree with any of them individually.

\section{Screening the Survey References}
\label{sec:ECSurveyScreen}

The other half of the frame is what the two surveys cite: \(313\) bibliography entries, \(305\)
distinct works once entries for the same work are merged, of which \(38\) are cited by both
(\cref{sec:ECSearch}). Two entries are the surveys citing each other and are set aside rather than
classified, leaving \(303\) works.

This half carries less information than the venue sweep, and the screen is correspondingly coarser.
A bibliography prints a title, a venue and a year; it prints no abstract, and a title in this
literature routinely describes a method while leaving the setting to the experiments
(\cref{sec:ECVenueScreen}). We therefore do not ask the triage question here. We ask a weaker one: is
this work \emph{plainly background} -- cited for context, and certainly not a paper that proposes,
analyses, benchmarks or applies methods in the area? A survey's bibliography is a closed set, so
unlike the venue sweep it needs no keyword gate; it is screened exhaustively.

\subsection{The Rule}

A work is classified as background only if it falls in one of six named categories: a textbook,
monograph or encyclopedic survey of a general field; a general machine-learning method,
architecture or training technique with no decision problem as its object; a general statistical or
estimation method with no downstream decision; general-purpose software, a solver manual or a
dataset release; a classical optimization algorithm or result with nothing learned or predicted in
it; or work in an unrelated field. A work plausibly proposing, analysing, benchmarking, surveying or
applying methods in the area is a candidate. Everything else is borderline.

Two features carry over from \cref{sec:ECVenueScreen}. The burden is on exclusion: background requires
that any expert in the area would agree without hesitation, and hesitation itself is a verdict.
And no work is excluded for reading like solver or machinery work, for the reason documented there.
Two features are new. A survey, tutorial or software library \emph{of this area} is a candidate
rather than background, since such works often carry benchmarks -- the two surveys from which this
frame is built are themselves an example. And the rule states explicitly that the model may use what it knows about a
cited work, since that is what any reader does with a reference string; we return to what this
reliance costs below.

\subsection{Implementation}

The implementation follows \cref{sec:ECVenueScreen}: the same pinned model identifier
\texttt{claude-opus-4-5-20251101}, greedy decoding, three runs with a majority bucket, and per
record a one-sentence ground in the model's own words, a self-reported confidence, the model
identifier, the prompt hash \texttt{37b31a8a9812} and the run date. The prompt is different because
the input and the question are different, and it is versioned in the replication repository as the
statement of the rule. Where a work is cited by both surveys, the model is shown both printed
strings at once.

The control set is built the same way and enforced the same way: \(20\) works known to be in scope,
none of which may come back as background, and \(6\) whose exclusion no reader would dispute, all of
which must. One detail is worth stating because it is easy to get wrong. A control set whose members
are named in the instrument it validates tests nothing, and both the worked examples and the
category lists in a prompt name papers. The script therefore asserts that no control work appears in
the prompt before it runs, and the examples were chosen from outside the frame for that reason. All
\(26\) controls pass.

\subsection{Outcome, and Why We Did Not Sample}

The screen assigns \(176\) works as candidates, \(85\) as background and \(42\) as borderline, with
\(302\) of the \(303\) unanimous across the three runs.

Here we depart from the venue-side design. There we measured the exclusion rate on a random sample,
because relabelling all \(221\) excluded records by hand was not a reasonable use of a reader.
Here the excluded pile is \(85\) works, each a single printed line, and reading all of them costs
about what reading a \(50\)-record sample costs on the venue side. One author therefore reviewed
every work the screen did not unanimously retain -- all \(85\) background, all \(42\) borderline,
\(127\) in total -- with the model's stated ground beside each reference string. A census returns a
count where a sample returns an interval, and we prefer the count.

Of the \(85\) background calls, none was overturned. We report that as it stands, with no confidence
statement attached: under one reader's application of the written rule, \(0\) of \(85\) exclusions
were wrong. The \(42\) borderline works were ruled \(32\) background and \(10\) candidate, leaving
\(186\) candidates and \(117\) background of the \(305\) works.

Those borderline rulings are a scope statement as much as a screening decision, and we record them
as such (\cref{sec:ECInclusion}). Ruled out: data-driven decision-making with no contextual
information, inverse optimization and inverse reinforcement learning, sequential decision-making,
and value-aware model learning. Ruled in: optimization proxies and learned solvers, which is the
same boundary the venue-side revision redrew, reached independently on the other half of the frame.

\subsection{Merging the Two Halves}

The two screens are applied to overlapping literatures and on two works they disagree: both were
ruled background here and retained by the venue triage. We retain them. An exclusion is terminal
only at full text, where the inclusion rules of \cref{sec:ECInclusion} are applied, and the two
screens ask deliberately different questions, so a disagreement between them is not a contradiction
to be resolved at this stage. The merged list is \(318\) papers: \(130\) found only by the venue
sweep, \(160\) cited only by the surveys, and \(28\) by both.

\subsection{Scope of the Claim}

Two limitations deserve to be stated plainly. The first is that a reference string is thin evidence,
and the rule leans on what a reader already knows about a cited work. That is a strength for the
papers this frame exists to capture -- a reader who recognizes \citet{berthet2020learning} from its
title does not need its abstract -- and a weakness everywhere else, since the same reliance is
invisible in the record. It is one reason the rule excludes nothing that is not plainly background.

The second is a deliberate trade against the venue-side design. The measurement of
\cref{sec:ECVenueScreen} was made blind: the model's bucket, ground and confidence were withheld from the
reader so that the reading was independent. This census was not. The reviewer saw the model's stated
ground beside each record, which is what made reading \(127\) works in one sitting practical, and
which also means the review is a check on stated reasoning rather than an independent relabelling.
We regard the coverage as worth the loss of independence at this stage -- a census of a small pile
with the grounds visible catches a class of error that a blind sample of the same pile would
mostly miss, namely a ground that is plausible in isolation and wrong for this literature -- but
the two numbers are not the same kind of number, and we do not pool them. An independent second
reader on a subsample of these records is owed, and we report it when it exists.
